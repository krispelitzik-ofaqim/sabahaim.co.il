What Is Specific Weight?

Imagine a tiny cube — one centimeter on every side, exactly the size of a sugar cube. Each time, we fill it with a different material: once iron, once copper, once gold. The cube is always the same size — but its weight changes! A cube of gold is far heavier than a cube of iron, because the atoms of gold are packed denser and heavier.

How much a cube like this weighs is the material’s "specific weight." It works like a fingerprint: every material has its own, and you can recognize it by that number alone — exactly as Robin did.
